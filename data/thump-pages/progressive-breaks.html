Progressive Breaks

Breaks slowed to a walk, mixed for a big room and given eight minutes to arrive.

Two bars at 131, straight, and the drums are deliberately the least interesting part. Three kick hits and three snare hits in bar one, three closed hats, one open. The shaker is the busy lane: sixteen hits in bar one, every sixteenth, alternating between velocity 40 and 54 so it reads as motion rather than as a percussion part. Bar two pulls the third kick back from the a of 3 to the and of 3, adds a fourth on the and of 4, puts a snare on the and of 4, and drops one shaker hit to mark the loop. The open hat moves from the and of 4 in bar one to the very last sixteenth in bar two, where it rings over the barline. If you want the record rather than the loop, the answer is a sixteen bar filter automation, not more drums.

What it is

Hybrid released Wide Angle in 1999 with an orchestra on it, and Way Out West had already spent three years proving that a breakbeat could hold a nine minute record. This is what happens when the breaks scene borrows progressive house's sense of time: the same 130ish tempo the rest of this family sits at, but arranged in eight-bar increments over eight minutes, with the drums deliberately kept plain so a filter sweep or a pad swell can be the event. The break is tight and quantised rather than swung. There is usually a single vocal line, drenched, arriving somewhere past the four minute mark. It was a DJ's genre first, built for the long mixes at Bedrock and Renaissance, and the structure follows from that.

What it sounds like

Wide, clean and patient. A tight compressed break with almost no room tone, a shaker running sixteenths under it, and a sub bass that sits so low it reads as pressure rather than a note. Above that, a filtered pad that takes sixteen bars to open, a delayed arpeggio in the far corners of the stereo field, and reverb tails long enough to overlap the next bar. Nothing is distorted. Almost everything is sidechained. It is mixed for a large room with a long decay time, which is why it sounds thin on a laptop and enormous through a proper rig.

Key elements

  • The shaker doing the workSixteenth notes throughout, quiet, alternating velocities. It supplies the forward motion so the drums do not have to, and that lets the kick and snare stay this sparse for this long.
  • A break that has been tidiedQuantised hard, gated, and stripped of its room. Progressive breaks is the one branch of this family that treats the break's human timing as a problem rather than the point.
  • Sixteen bar filter movesA low-pass on the pad opening across sixteen bars, then closing across eight. The whole arrangement is a sequence of these, and there is often no melodic change at all underneath.
  • Sidechain as a rhythmEverything ducked to the kick with a 200ms release, long enough that the pump is audible as a pulse. The breathing is doing rhythmic work that no drum in the pattern is doing.
  • The late vocalOne line, often a single word, arriving after four minutes with a long reverb and a quarter-note delay. It is a structural marker rather than a song, and it never repeats more than a few times.

How to get them

  • Quantise the break, then rebuild the feelSnap every hit to the grid, then take the two or three ghost snares back off the grid by 10ms. You want machine tightness with a couple of human hits in it, which is the opposite of the funky breaks approach.
  • Program the shaker across both barsEvery sixteenth, alternating velocity 40 and 54, with one hit removed and the last four pushed up in the second bar. Randomise the timing by 2ms and no more. If the shaker sounds like a shaker rather than a texture it is too loud.
  • Sub, not basslineOne sine, low-passed at 80Hz, held for two bars at a time. Any note movement above 100Hz belongs to a separate mid bass layer that you can filter independently.
  • Set the sidechain release long180 to 220ms on the pad, the bass and the reverb returns. At 131 that is nearly a whole beat, so the pump becomes the second rhythm in the track and covers the fact the drums barely move.
  • Arrange in sixteens and be patientIntro of thirty two bars, a drop at ninety six, the vocal past bar one hundred and sixty. If the track is under seven minutes it will not do its job in a mix, which is what it was built for.
  • Reverb longer than the barA 4 second plate on the snare with 40ms pre-delay, high-passed at 500Hz on the return. A tail that overlaps into the next bar is what makes the gaps between phrases sound deliberate.

Reference points

Hybrid, Way Out West, Burufunk, Momu, Adam Freeland's slower material, and the Distinctive and Hope labels. Listed as reference points for the sound rather than as endorsements.

The pattern is a starting point rather than a rule. If you want the theory underneath why any of it works, Battuto measures it.
