Hard Drum

Percussion doing all of the work, with the melody and the four-four thrown out.

Two bars at 132, true tempo, and the density is the point. There is no backbeat on 2 anywhere in this pattern: the clang takes beat 4 in bar one with a soft answer on the and of 4, and in bar two it goes to the e of 3, beat 4 and the a of 4. The kick holds beat 1 in both bars and then behaves like a drum rather than a pulse, three hits in bar one against five in bar two. Five conga hits in bar one, on the a of 1, the e of 2, beat 3, the and of 3 and the e of 4. The timbale answers underneath on the e of 1, the a of 2 and the e of 3, then moves in bar two to the e of 1, the a of 2, the and of 3 and the and of 4. Closed hats are almost absent on purpose. Only two steps in the whole loop have nothing on them at all, and both of them are beat 2, which is exactly the beat this style refuses to mark.

What it is

Endgame attached the phrase hard drum to this around 2017, describing what he and the Bala Club crowd in London were making and playing: club tracks built from drums and nothing else, borrowing rhythmic shapes from dancehall, gqom, kuduro and baile funk while leaving the chords, the riff and the vocal hook behind. TSVI and Wallwork were running Nervous Horizon on a version of the same idea. Kelman Duran worked it from dembow in New York. Nazar in Manchester pointed it at Angolan kuduro and the war his family lived through, and called what came out rough kuduro. Tempo settles near 130 because that is roughly where those source rhythms meet. The discipline is negative: no pad, no lead, no build. If a hard drum track has a development section, it is a conga moving one sixteenth.

What it sounds like

Dry, close and loud. Congas, timbales, woodblocks and rototoms recorded with barely any room, plus a snare that has been layered with a metal clang so it cuts without needing reverb. Kicks are short and tuned rather than long and subby, because six percussion parts at 132 leave no space for a tail. The samples carry the meaning: a gunshot, a cocking sound, a knife on a steel, a whistle, a shout. Sub bass, when it arrives, is one held note under the whole eight bars rather than a line. Nothing is wet. If a producer wants distance they use a shorter sample rather than a longer reverb.

Key elements

  • The kick is one drum of severalIt holds beat 1 and then goes wherever the rhythm needs it, three hits in one bar and five in the next. It is not the pulse and it does not carry the track.
  • Two percussion lanes arguingA conga part and a timbale part written separately and left to collide. Neither is the groove on its own, and the interlocking is the composition.
  • A clang instead of a snareThe backbeat sound is metal: a struck plate, a pipe, a sampled machine. It lands on 4 rather than on 2 and 4, which is the single fastest way to stop a beat feeling like house.
  • Sample choice as contentGunshots, sirens, whistles and blade sounds carry the track's meaning because there is no melody or vocal to carry it. Choosing them carelessly is the main way this style goes wrong.
  • No melodic layer at allNot a sparse melody, not a subtle one. None. Every hard drum track that adds a pad turns immediately into ordinary club music with good percussion.

How to get them

  • Record the percussion yourselfA conga, a cowbell and a chunk of scaffolding, close-miked in a dead room, beats any sample pack here. The style lives on transients and pack samples arrive pre-reverbed.
  • Tune the drums to each otherPitch the conga, the timbale and the clang so they sit a fourth and a seventh apart. With no harmony in the track the drums are the harmony, and three untuned percussion parts sound like a mess rather than a chord.
  • Write the two perc lanes apartProgram the conga over eight bars with everything else muted, then do the timbale the same way, then unmute both. Writing them together produces a single part in two timbres.
  • KickShort, tuned around 60Hz, decay under 150 milliseconds, no click layer. It has to leave the midrange free because everything else you own is in the midrange.
  • One sub note per sectionA single sine held for eight bars, sidechained to the kick by about 4dB. A moving bassline turns this back into a genre with a bottom half.
  • Cut the reverb, keep the gunshot dryHigh-pass any reverb return at 800Hz and keep it under 10 percent wet. The sample-based hits should sound like they were fired in the room you are in.

Reference points

Endgame and the Bala Club crew, TSVI and Wallwork on Nervous Horizon, Kelman Duran, Nazar, Why Be, LSDXOXO and Toxe. Named as reference points for the sound, not as endorsements or as anyone to imitate.

The pattern is a starting point rather than a rule. If you want the theory underneath why any of it works, Battuto measures it.
