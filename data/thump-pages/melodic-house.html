Thump

Melodic House

Four on the floor, and everything else pointed at the feeling.

The kick is on every beat and the clap doubles it on 2 and 4. The open hat sits on every offbeat eighth, which is the part that makes it house. The closed hat only ticks on the beat, under the kick, and the shaker carries the offbeat sixteenths - splitting the roll and the pulse between two elements rather than piling both onto the hats. Bar two is identical until the last beat, where the final open hat is dropped and the closed hats lift into the downbeat, so the two-bar loop has an audible seam.

What it is

House that puts the harmony and the atmosphere in front, and keeps the drums steady underneath rather than making them the event. It came out of progressive and deep house in the 2010s and it is what most people now mean by the sound of Anjunadeep or Afterlife. The rhythm section is deliberately unsurprising, because its job is to hold still while a chord progression takes eight or sixteen bars to arrive somewhere.

What it sounds like

Wide and unhurried. A kick with real weight but no aggression, a clap soaked in reverb, and a hi-hat pattern that ticks along without asking for attention. Over that: pads that take bars to open, a plucked arpeggio bouncing on a dotted delay, and a bass that breathes in and out with the kick. It is hypnotic rather than peaky, and the emotional register is closer to wistful than euphoric.

Key elements

  • Four on the floorA kick on every beat. Non-negotiable, and the reason everything else can be loose.
  • The offbeat open hatAn open hi-hat on the and of each beat, ringing until the next kick cuts it off. This is the single element that makes a pattern sound like house rather than techno.
  • Clap on 2 and 4A clap rather than a snare, usually with a long reverb tail that fills the space until the next kick.
  • SidechainEverything harmonic ducking to the kick. The pumping is not a side effect, it is the groove: it turns a static pad into something that moves.
  • A progression with somewhere to goEight or sixteen bars of minor chords that resolve slowly. The drums stay flat so the harmony can carry the arrangement.

How to get them

  • KickPick one with its weight around 50Hz and a short click on top so it survives on phones. Tune it to the root of your key - a kick that disagrees with the bass is the most common reason a mix sounds muddy.
  • Open hatLet it ring longer than you think, then cut it with the next kick. If your sampler chokes it automatically, set the hat and the closed hat to the same choke group.
  • ClapSend it to a plate or hall reverb at around two seconds, then high-pass the return above 300Hz so the tail does not sit on the bass.
  • SidechainA compressor keyed to the kick, fast attack and a release just short of a quarter note, or a volume shaper if you want the pump to be exact. Duck the bass hardest, then the pads, and leave the hats alone.
  • BassA sine or triangle sub with a little saturation for the mids. Keep it mono below 120Hz and let it follow the root of each chord rather than playing a line of its own.
  • PadsDetuned saws, a slow filter opening over eight bars, and more reverb than feels sensible. The pad is the arrangement.

Reference points

Lane 8, Yotto, Tale of Us, Ben Bohmer, Anjunadeep and Afterlife as labels. Named as reference points for the sound, not as endorsements or as anyone to imitate.

The pattern is a starting point rather than a rule. If you want the theory underneath why any of it works, Battuto measures it.
